The goal wasn't to build a dashboard for its own sake - it was to answer a real business question: how can Dillard's improve profitability across its Illinois stores while making smarter operational decisions?
    bullets:
      - Team of four - Umang Gupta, Andrea Southwick, Saurav Pandit, and Landon Obroy.

  - id: questions
    heading: Three questions driving the analysis
    body: >-
      The team focused the analysis around three specific business questions rather than a general performance overview.
    bullets:
      - Which Dillard's Illinois stores were performing the strongest?
      - Which departments were contributing most to profit?
      - Which store or department level changes could reduce operating costs and improve profitability?
  - id: findings
    heading: What the data showed
    body: >-
      Store-level and department-level profitability told two different stories - the weakest store by profit wasn't necessarily the right one to close, once market context was factored in.
    bullets:
      - Store 306 was the strongest performer - roughly $1.52M in profit, 47.03% of the total across all three stores, in a higher-income customer area.
      - Store 349 had the lowest total profit (~$0.47M, 14.62% of the total), but wasn't recommended for closure first due to its market context and redevelopment potential.
      - Store 310 - despite outperforming Store 349 on raw profit - was identified as the stronger closure candidate once mall decline and market conditions were factored in.
      - Leased departments were a consistent weak point across all three stores, with several micro-categories running at negative or near-zero profitability.
  - id: recommendation
    heading: The recommendation
    body: >-
      The analysis closed with a concrete two-part strategy, not just a set of observations.
    bullets:
      - Close or repurpose underperforming leased departments across the Illinois stores.
      - Evaluate Store 310 for closure based on profitability, mall conditions, and market alignment.
      - Combined estimated impact - approximately $1.65M to $1.7M in annual profitability improvement.
