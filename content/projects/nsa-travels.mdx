title: NSA Travels
summary: A real-world case study documenting architecture, rollout, training, and business impact for a travel product transformation.
problem: The product had grown into a brittle, hand-operated workflow that slowed down publishing, support, and change delivery.
solution: I rebuilt the content and delivery flow around a typed MDX pipeline, clearer boundaries, and a deployment process the team could trust.
architectureNotes: The system keeps routing, rendering, and content validation separate so the business logic stays framework-independent and the content layer can evolve without rewriting the app.
techStack:
  - Next.js 15
  - React 19
  - TypeScript
  - MDX
  - Zod
challenges:
  - Keeping content authoring flexible without losing type safety.
  - Preserving production-grade deployment behavior while migrating the content model.
  - Maintaining clean dependency boundaries across presentation and infrastructure.
lessonsLearned:
  - Typed content pipelines catch bad content earlier than manual QA.
  - A small, explicit repository abstraction is easier to maintain than a framework-heavy data layer.
  - Production readiness depends on both architecture and operational clarity.
