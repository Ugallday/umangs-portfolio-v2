sections:
  - id: hook
    heading: The books my family's travel agency actually runs on
    body: >-
      Every document here — a purchase, a sale, a return, a payment — is written as a balanced journal entry. The day book, the registers, the VAT return and the final report are all views over that one ledger, so they cannot disagree with each other. Getting to that took two rewrites.
    bullets:
      - Double-entry from the ground up, not totals bolted onto a spreadsheet.
      - "Three versions: 22 copied HTML pages, then a shared core, then a multi-tenant product."
      - Works fully offline. Syncing to PostgreSQL is optional.
  - id: problem
    heading: Version one disagreed with itself
    body: >-
      v1 was 22 standalone pages, each carrying its own copy of the navigation, the CSS and the arithmetic. A review of it recorded eight defects. Two pages calculated VAT by different formulas and reported different figures for the same money.
    bullets:
      - Two VAT engines shipping inside one product.
      - Reports joined on party name, so renaming a customer detached their history.
      - localStorage capped the business at roughly 5 MB. The real day book is 23 MB.
      - Importing two years of history took over seven minutes, one record at a time.
  - id: what-was-built
    heading: One shell over a framework-free core
    body: >-
      I replaced the approach rather than patching the defects. No view computes a balance now; it asks ledger.js for figures and asks the component kit to draw them. The core modules never touch the DOM.
    bullets:
      - Nine voucher types over one fixed chart of accounts.
      - Seven reports, every one of them a sum over the same postings.
      - Arithmetic computed once at posting time and stored, never recomputed per screen.
      - One shell replaced roughly 700 KB across 22 pages. No build step, and it runs from a USB stick.
  - id: correctness
    heading: Three invariants the ledger refuses to break
    body: >-
      An unbalanced voucher never saves. post() sums both sides and throws if they differ by more than half a paisa, and no code path into the ledger skips that check. Totals are derived from their components rather than read off the form.
    bullets:
      - The ticketing VAT split is verified against the real supplier day book, to the paisa.
      - Rounding drift is bounded and explained; the three parts always re-add to the bill.
      - Cancelling a voucher removes its ledger effect but keeps its number, because a tax sequence must not develop holes.
      - A health check re-derives every invariant across the whole database on demand.
  - id: multi-tenant
    heading: One deployment, every client, separated by the database
    body: >-
      Every row carries a tenant id, and every table has row-level security keyed to the tenant id inside the caller's signed token rather than to anything the browser can edit. PostgreSQL answers "whose books are these" on every single request.
    bullets:
      - Composite foreign keys mean a row cannot physically point across a tenant boundary.
      - Closed years are enforced three times over — in the guard, in the ledger, and by a database trigger.
      - Five ranked roles. Nobody can grant a role above their own, and the last holder of a tier cannot be removed.
      - Onboarding a client is a vendor console entry and one activation key.
  - id: credentials
    heading: Deleting my own password code
    body: >-
      v3 hashed staff passwords in the browser: a salt, 12,000 rounds of SHA-256, and a check against Node's crypto implementation. It worked, and it was still the wrong place for it. Supabase Auth owns the credential now, and the migration drops the old columns instead of quietly leaving them behind.
    bullets:
      - "Dropped outright: salt, password_hash, failed_attempts, locked_until."
      - A leaked backup should not carry credential material for logins that no longer open anything.
      - The app keeps only what it owns — whether a password is still temporary, and when it last changed.
      - Changing a password now ends the session and sends you back to sign in.
  - id: offline-sync
    heading: Local-first, because the connection is not guaranteed
    body: >-
      IndexedDB is the working copy every screen reads, which is why the app still works on a bad line in a Kathmandu office. PostgreSQL is the durable copy the machines converge on. A cycle pushes before it pulls, so the server can never discard an edit that had simply not uploaded yet.
    bullets:
      - Deletes are tombstoned rather than dropped, so they reach the other machines.
      - Voucher counters merge by maximum. Skipping a number is harmless; repeating one is not.
      - Records map to real columns, so the books can be queried in SQL.
      - Losing the connection queues changes. It does not stop the app.
  - id: sync-hardening
    heading: Teaching a failed push to fail quietly
    body: >-
      A cycle used to send every table together, so one table refused by a policy stranded the whole queue behind it. Each table now settles on its own, and the outbox retries only what actually failed.
    bullets:
      - One refused table no longer blocks the tables queued behind it.
      - Rows are grouped by column shape, so a single odd row cannot fail a batch.
      - Fiscal years push insert-only, which is what their row-level security allows.
      - The exempt-column list is derived from the data rather than maintained by hand.
  - id: performance
    heading: The migration that proved the rewrite
    body: >-
      I pointed v3 at the agency's real Tally export — 29,825 rows, two years of trading — and analysed it before writing a single row. Voucher counts by type, the period covered, the parties it would create, and whether the source itself balanced.
    bullets:
      - 7,445 vouchers, 1,084 parties and 8 new accounts created. 0 rows skipped.
      - ₨845,644,371.27 of source debits posted, with the trial balance tying exactly.
      - Every voucher balanced on write, and the health check re-derived the invariants across the whole database afterwards.
      - "The rewrite is what made that possible: load once into memory, validate, batch the writes, flush once."
  - id: testing
    heading: 150 assertions, no test framework
    body: >-
      The core has no DOM dependencies, so it runs headlessly under Node against a small window stand-in. There is no test framework for the same reason there is no build step — it has to keep working on a machine with nothing installed.
    bullets:
      - The sync suite runs three app instances against an in-memory PostgREST stand-in.
      - It asserts the third client sees none of the other's data, and is refused when it forges a tenant id.
      - The document suite checks the hand-written PDF byte by byte, cross-reference offsets included.
      - What is not covered is stated in the README rather than left to be discovered.
  - id: outcome
    heading: What changed for the business
    body: >-
      The agency's books moved off spreadsheets and paper onto a system where the numbers agree by construction and a filed year cannot quietly change. v3 also made it sellable: there is no per-client build, so a new business is a console entry away.
    bullets:
      - Replaced the Excel and paper bookkeeping described in the NSA Travels case study.
      - Reports export to Excel with live formulas, so totals recalculate rather than arrive frozen.
      - Documents print, flatten to an image, and email as a PDF built without a PDF library.
      - Suspending a client is enforced by the database while their app keeps working locally.
  - id: reflection
    heading: What building it taught me
    body: >-
      An invariant you only document is not an invariant. Every rule that actually holds in this system holds because something refuses to proceed when it is broken, usually in more than one place. The rules I was merely careful about in v1 are the ones that broke.
    bullets:
      - Enforce constraints where they cannot be routed around — the database, not the form.
      - Derive values rather than storing them twice. v1's worst defect was two copies of one number.
      - Measure a rewrite against real data, not a fixture that flatters it.
      - Write down what the system does not do.
  - id: whats-next
    heading: What comes next
    body: >-
      The roadmap tracks real gaps rather than a wish list. Nepal's tax year runs on the Bikram Sambat calendar and the IRD expects its own register formats, so that is the compliance work ahead.
    bullets:
      - Bikram Sambat conversion, BS date pickers and BS-month VAT periods.
      - IRD-format sales and purchase registers.
      - Supabase Realtime, so changes land without waiting for the next sync cycle.
      - Per-record locking to replace last-write-wins, and multi-branch support inside one tenant.
